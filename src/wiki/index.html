Jaime Chapinal

Software engineer, independent app maker and Kinect research co-author

Jaime Chapinal Cervantes is a software engineer whose public profile lists work at Spotify and independent app development under Chapiware. His personal website describes a senior engineering role on Spotify’s web and desktop clients.[1][2]

His public record also includes a 2012 paper on Kinect interaction, a game-development conference talk and a collection of apps for photos, reading and everyday tracking.[7][9][3] Google Play identifies Chapiware’s Ayer developer as Jaime Chapinal Cervantes, linking the full name to the product portfolio.[4]

Education and research#

Chapinal’s website lists computing studies at Universidad Carlos III de Madrid (2007–11), software-development and human–computer interaction studies at Granada (2011–12), and video-game development at Universidad Complutense de Madrid (2012–13).[1]

A University of Granada record independently names him as the author of a master’s project on cognitive rehabilitation through video games using Kinect, defended on 23 July 2012 and supervised by Francisco Luis Gutiérrez Vela and Patricia Paderewski Rodríguez.[6]

PUBLICATION · 2012

Natural interaction techniques using Kinect

Jaime Chapinal Cervantes, Francisco Luis Gutiérrez Vela and Patricia Paderewski-Rodríguez.

Published in the proceedings of the 13th International Conference on Interacción Persona-Ordenador, held in Elche, Spain, 3–5 October 2012. The paper examines adapting 3D interaction techniques to Kinect and presents a cognitive-rehabilitation case study.[7][8]

Public work and writing#

His website links to the GitHub account Xatpy, whose profile links back to the same site. Public repositories include Send to X4, its mobile app, Beatable, a La Vida Moderna soundboard and a project connecting Strava data to CartoDB maps.[1][10]

An attendee’s account of Freakend Juegos 2016 links to Chapinal’s presentation on internationalization best practices. This is a useful trace of his work outside his own résumé.[9]

His blog’s 2026 entries discuss building a hug-timer app while running, organizing an iPhone photo archive, developing an app through voice interaction from a car, and creating a children’s comic from a family photo. These are self-published accounts of projects, not independent reports.[11]

He publicly lists video games, programming, GIFs, football and The Office among his interests.[1]

The experiment behind this page#

On 6 September 2026, Jack Friks posted that he had asked an agent to independently research him and create a wiki. He described the result as accurate, unsettling and enjoyable; the attached image showed an encyclopedic article with an infobox and citations.[12]

One retrievable reaction, from Creao AI, framed it as a test of how much of someone’s life is already publicly reconstructable.[13] The full reply and quote-post conversation could not be reviewed, so this page does not claim to summarize its overall reception.

This article applies that idea to Jaime Chapinal: public sources supply the biography, while the evidence notes show where a plausible story would outrun the record. Research used publicly accessible pages only, without private accounts, messages or workspace material as biographical evidence.
